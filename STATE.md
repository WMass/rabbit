## 1. Unbinned likelihood terms — `rabbit/unbinned.py`

rabbit's native likelihood is binned (Poisson / chi2 over bins) plus the
quadratic external terms of `rabbit/external_likelihood.py`. This branch adds a
third additive contribution,

```
NLL_unbinned(x) = - sum_i log L_i(x)
```

over `n` **candidates** rather than bins. A term is a plain differentiable TF
function of a *named slice* of the fit parameter vector, so value, gradient,
Hessian and HVPs come off the same tapes the binned likelihood uses and every
downstream consumer — minimiser, EDM, covariance, impacts, scans, snapshots,
result file — works unchanged. An unbinned card is an **ordinary rabbit fit**.

`UnbinnedTerm` is the base; the two concrete terms are:

**`MassCFTerm`** — each candidate's density is the inverse Fourier transform of
a product of characteristic functions,

```
L_i = 1/(pi sigma_i) Int_0^inf Re[ phi_K(t/sigma_i) phi_res(t/sigma_i)
                                   e^{S_i(t)} e^{-i t (m_i - m_pred_i)/sigma_i} ] dt
```

with `phi_K` an empirical tabulated kernel CF (FSR), `phi_res` the physics
kernel of the resonance, and `S_i(t)` the per-candidate CVH log-CF exponent.

**`MaterialCFTerm`** (subclass) — the same density, but `sigma_i` and the
log-CF exponent are **functions of the fit's own physical parameters** rather
than constants: the per-group material amounts and the per-hit-class variance
scales. One fit then floats one set of parameters across the width and the mean
of the mass term and the quadratic hit-chi2 term.

### Physics kernels

| class | resonance | CF |
|---|---|---|
| `DeltaKernel` | J/psi, K_S | 1 |
| `BreitWignerKernel` | Upsilon | `e^{-Gamma|t|/2}`, analytic |
| `TabulatedLineshapeKernel` | Z/gamma\* | numerical, from a provider |

The Z provider is `rabbit/lineshapes/zgamma.py` (`ZGammaLineshape`): the Born
NC Drell-Yan spectrum folded with a parton-luminosity table
(`rabbit/lineshapes/data/zlumi_nnpdf31_nnlo_13tev.npz`, regenerated by
`make_lumi_table.py`), the FSR kernel and the acceptance, differentiable in the
POIs `m_Z` and `Gamma_Z`, with a smooth `K(m)` shape floated on top.

The FSR fold is one constant `(nm, n_born)` matrix either way, but `fsr=` takes
two representations of the kernel, dispatched on the keys of the mapping or
npz. **Atoms** `(r, w, m_lo, m_hi)` are point masses at `r = m_post/m_pre`,
optionally banded in `m_pre`: piecewise constant in mass, and merged in `u` at
a `var_budget` / `sigma_cap`. A **table** `(m_nodes, u_edges, K, u_mean, p0)`
is the cell-integrated probability mass of `u = -ln(m_post/m_pre)` at a ladder
of mass nodes, and has neither discretisation: the rows are interpolated onto
every Born grid point (no bands) and every cell is deposited whole, by the rule
that is exact for its width — a point mass at the cell's first moment while the
cell is narrower than the output spacing, the exact hat-basis projection of its
own density once it is wider (no `sigma_cap`). Both are serialised in the card,
a table by reference when it came from an npz that is still on disk and inline
(zlib'd base64) otherwise. `calibration_studies/zchannel/fsr_table.py` writes
the tables and `tests/test_zgamma_kernel.py` test 8 is the validation: a table
of point-like cells reproduces the atom fold matrix to 1e-10 and its pdf to
7e-13, the mass interpolation converges as the square of the node spacing
(3.6e-4 / 8.2e-5 / 2.0e-5 / 4.8e-6 at 4 / 2 / 1 / 0.5 GeV; cubic at 4 GeV is
3.0e-7), and both deposit rules reproduce their own naive transcription
exactly.

### The v form

`m_Z` and `Gamma_Z` are fitted in the **v formulation**: the convolution is
done in `v = Int dm/m^p` and the candidate is conditioned on `k = sigma/m^p`,
not on `sigma`. This exists because conditioning on the resolution while
fitting the mass **pairs** the two (Punzi) and biases the mass; `vpow` is
carried through the card round trip.

### The two mass corrections

Both are mandatory and both are first-principles — no free parameter:

1. **the sigma artefact** (`self_consistent_sigma`) — `sigma_fit` depends on
   the fluctuation the candidate actually had, so a likelihood that conditions
   on it is Neyman-biased;
2. **the exact Jensen (second-order) term** (`jensen_mode="exact"`) — the
   `1/2 tr(H Sigma)` term of the predicted mass, in its exact rather than
   mean-shift form; the mean-shift form over-corrects by 27–44 %.

Both are bounded to their domain of validity: `corr_clip`, `corr_form`
(`residual` / `fluctuation`), `corr_coeff_max` on the quadratic coefficient and
`corr_a_max` on the first-order one, overridable per fit with
`--unbinnedCorrCoeffMax` / `--unbinnedCorrAMax`. A **delta-kernel** term takes
the **exact residual form** rather than a truncated expansion
(`--unbinnedDeltaKernelForm`, default `auto`).

### Truncated normalisation

Cutting on a residual obliges the term to normalise over what it cut to. A term
carries its window (`norm_window`, in mass units, or `norm_window_sigma` for a
pull cut) and divides by the truncated integral, which is done **in Fourier
space**, not on a mass grid. For a `MaterialCFTerm` that normalisation
**depends on the parameters** (they move `sigma_i`), so it is differentiated,
not cached; where a family of norms is genuinely fixed it is declared as such.

### Backgrounds

`UniformBackground` and `BernsteinBackground`, with a floating fraction.

## 2. Declaring the parameters

An unbinned term consumes fit parameters **by name**, so something must put
those names in the fit vector (`[ParamModel params | systs]`):

* `--paramModel UnbinnedParams` — reads name, start value, Gaussian prior and
  POI flag out of the card's `unbinned_terms` group, so the parameter list
  cannot drift from the term that uses it. Tokens: `poi:`, `terms:`.
* `--paramModel ExternalParams` — the same for an **external** (quadratic
  `g`, `H`) term, read from an `auxiliary` bundle written next to it.

Cards are written with `TensorWriter.add_unbinned_term()` /
`add_auxiliary()` / `add_dummy_channel()`; `rabbit/inputdata.py` reads the group
back. `pick_models.py` (in `calibration_studies/resolution/matres/`) prints the
right `--paramModel` for a given card.

## 3. Scale: chunking, sharding, snapshots

**Chunking.** The candidate loop lives *inside the term*
(`UnbinnedTerm._chunk_loop`: a `tf.while_loop` inside `tf.custom_gradient`,
twice, `parallel_iterations=1`, so exactly one chunk is live). Without it the
term builds the whole sample's forward tape and the `pfor` Hessian keeps
`nparams` copies of it — 116 GB at 300 k candidates and 5 parameters.
`--unbinnedChunk` sets the size, `--unbinnedChunkMode graph|eager` the loop.
`ChunkTable` / `JacChunkTable` hold the per-chunk state and accept a *traced*
chunk index; the whole-sample sparse `D` is built in an `init_scope`, and the
per-candidate `D` is stored **dense** because `sparse_dense_matmul` has no
deterministic GPU kernel.

**Sharding.** `--nDevices N` (and `--devices` to pin them) runs a bins-sharded
likelihood on `MultiDeviceFitter` (`rabbit/sharding.py`). Two structural rules,
established on 4 GPUs: backward placement must be explicit (one tape per shard,
differentiating w.r.t. a device-local copy of `x`, else the placer drags every
shard's `logk` across the bus — 6.5 ms -> 135 ms at 4 shards), and XLA clusters
are single-device, so each shard is jit-compiled alone and the combiner stays a
plain graph. Only the GPUs a fit actually uses are occupied, preferring
unoccupied ones. Postfit steps that are not sharded are refused rather than run
wrong. `make_fitter()` picks the subclass from `--nDevices` and forwards all
kwargs, so it cannot drift from `Fitter.__init__`.

**The dense Hessian** is assembled from **batched HVPs** (`--hvpBatch`, default
256) rather than from the vectorised jacobian — this is also what makes a
preconditioner reference matrix affordable on a large card.

**Snapshots** (`--snapshotFile` / `--snapshotInterval`, upstream #155, carried here): the callback is the
only place that sees the accepted iterate every iteration, so it writes them;
a snapshot is always stored in **physical** coordinates (a preconditioned
iterate would load without complaint and be wrong).

## 4. Minimiser and frozen parameters

The native TF minimisers `tf-trust-exact`, `tf-trust-ncg`, `tf-trust-krylov`
(upstream #153, originally from this work) avoid the scipy host round trip; a
subproblem that **cannot predict descent is not a converged fit** and says so.

**Frozen parameters.** `--freezeParameters` freezes the **step**, not only the
gradient: the frozen subspace is *removed from the vector the minimiser sees*
(`restrict` / `expand` around every minimiser call) rather than merely
stop-gradiented. A stop-gradiented parameter leaves an exactly zero Hessian row
and column, the trust-region subproblem is handed a singular matrix, meets the
hard case every iteration and converges linearly;
`Fitter.hess_for_minimizer` additionally puts 1 on the frozen diagonal (exact,
since the frozen gradient components are zero). One benchmark: 66 iterations at
EDM 83 and still crawling -> **18 iterations at EDM 1.0e-15**.

An **unconstrained but floating** parameter also has a zero Hessian row, and
that one is physics: `Fitter.warn_unconstrained` names it, testing the **row**,
not the diagonal (the diagonal test fires on the POIs of any card mixing a
hit-chi2 term of curvature 7e13 with mass POIs at 0.06).

**Preconditioning** (upstream #156/#157, in since this merge). `--precondition`
reparameterises blocks of the parameter vector by a factorisation of a
reference Hessian, so the trust region is spherical in the new coordinates.
`--preconditionTransform spectral` whitens by `|H| = Q|Lambda|Q^T`, giving every
eigendirection its own scale and **keeping the sign of negative curvature**
(which trust-krylov exploits to escape saddles); `ridge` (the default, kept for
backward compatibility) adds a scalar ridge and cannot serve an indefinite
block. A block whose true condition number the transform would make worse is
**dropped**, and the log reports the true condition number before -> after.
`--stallRelTol` makes the early-stopping test relative; with `--maxRestarts`
the transform is rebuilt at the point actually reached. Preconditioning a
**frozen** parameter is refused — the two operations do not commute.
What it buys, measured on two 60-parameter CPU cards: the block whitens from
1.03e8 / 7.78e7 to 1, an already-converged fit reproduces to every printed
digit with its EDM 5.6e-12 -> 2.1e-16, a fit whose Hessian is singular for want
of information is improved but not rescued, and a direction with no interior
minimum is walked out to its boundary rather than hidden. RESOLUTION.md §3.3.

**On the GPU.** The preconditioned path runs on the device exactly as the plain
one does: objective, gradient, Hessian and HVPs are the same traced
`tf.function`s, and the transform itself is small numpy at the scipy boundary
(one dense `[npar, npar]` factorisation per build, `T`/`T^T` per call).
Measured on `z_vzero.hdf5`, H200, 12 `trust-exact` iterations: plain 72 s,
preconditioned 73 s, GPU 70-90 % across the fitting phase in both (the ~35 s
before it is import, card load and tracing, at 0 %). The plain fit is
bit-identical with the preconditioner compiled in and switched off:
`nllvalreduced` 150648.54430236336, `edmval` 128.6403736418085 and all eleven
parameters to the last digit. The same preconditioned fit forced onto the CPU
takes 1258 s against 73 s and lands in the same place to float64 rounding:
`nllvalreduced` 1.9e-13 relative, `edmval` 1.4e-9, every parameter within
2e-8 of its own sigma. At a FIXED point -- the reference Hessian of the
`z_V_s7` control, where no trajectory divergence can accumulate -- CPU and GPU
give `edmval` 9427.109084638501 vs 9427.109084639122, 6.6e-14 relative.

A preconditioned fit that reports **0 % / 0 MiB** on the GPU is an environment
fault, not the transform. `setup_env_engaging.sh` puts the CUDA wheel lib dirs
on `LD_LIBRARY_PATH` with a glob; a job script that sources it under `set -f`
-- which `precond_refit.sbatch` did, because `--preconditionParams .*` has to
word-split unquoted -- leaves the loader path without CUDA, TF then skips
registering the GPU at a log level `TF_CPP_MIN_LOG_LEVEL=2` hides, and the fit
runs on the CPU at about 1/40 the speed (0.6 Hessians/h against the 22-27/h of
the certified GPU runs). Both ends are fixed (`calibration_studies` 6e320d3:
the env script restores globbing around its own loop, the sbatch sources the
environment before turning globbing off), and `rabbit_fit.py` logs the devices
TF sees at startup, warning when it was built with CUDA and has none.

## 6. How a fit is certified

Every fit goes through `rabbit_fit.py`, and is quoted only if **all three** of
these hold (RESOLUTION.md §3):

1. **the value** — it returns the number the card is known to give;
2. **the NLL** — it is the *same* minimum. Two converged fits of one card whose
   NLL differs by more than float noise are at different stationary points
   (measured: 81.6 NLL units apart, with the *smaller* EDM at the *worse*
   point). NLL is compared only across fits of the same model.
3. **the EDM** — rabbit's own `edmval` = `1/2 g^T H^-1 g` with the FULL
   Hessian, **< 1e-3**. A displacement of `d` sigma in the worst direction
   costs `d^2/2`, so EDM < 1e-3 means `d` < 0.045 sigma.

`|grad|_inf` and per-parameter diagonal Newton proxies are **not** convergence
tests and have retracted numbers before. EDM certifies stationarity, not
optimality, so it is never quoted alone.
